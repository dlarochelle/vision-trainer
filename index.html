Perceptual learning · experimental

See better. Read faster.

The goal is better vision and faster reading. We are testing whether it works; more testing is needed. Two measurements: contrast sensitivity under lateral masking, and comprehension under rapid serial presentation.

display scaled

What to look for. Each trial shows two flashes in a row, labelled 1 and 2 in the corner. Both flashes show the same two high-contrast striped patches, one above centre and one below centre — ignore those, they never change. In exactly one of the two flashes a third, much fainter striped patch appears in the gap between them, at the centre of the screen. Your only job is to say which flash had that faint centre patch.
Press F/← if it was in flash 1, J/→ if it was in flash 2. The centre patch gets fainter as you go, so you will reach a point where you genuinely cannot see it — guess then. Guessing is expected; the staircase needs your wrong answers to find your threshold.
Stop if you get a headache, eye strain, or double vision.

Display scale magnifies the stimulus, which lowers the angular frequency the observer sees to the nominal value divided by the scale; points are plotted at that effective frequency, so a 3 c/deg block at 4× plots at 0.75 c/deg. This is a demonstration of the paradigm at a small fraction of any studied training dose: published protocols run roughly 900–1,000 trials per session over 30–40 sessions; a block here is a few dozen trials.

Deviation from the published protocols, stated plainly: they do not sweep fixed spatial frequencies (Polat starts low and advances adaptively; Zhou et al. train at the individual's cutoff frequency). This app sweeps 3 / 6 / 12 c/deg because it is measuring a contrast sensitivity function, not replicating a training protocol.

Degrees are derived from an assumed 96 CSS px/inch display and the viewing distance above. Display scale magnifies the drawn stimulus for visibility; the px/degree readout stays at the true, unscaled value, so magnification is never disguised as angular size. If the browser also downscales the canvas to fit the window, that factor is reported on its own line and folded into the plotted frequency. Thresholds are in 8-bit pixel contrast, not gamma-linearized luminance; comparable within one display, not across displays.

This mode measures comprehension under RSVP (rapid serial visual presentation). It does not measure, and is not known to improve, natural reading. Rayner et al. (2016, Psychological Science in the Public Interest) concluded that RSVP gains do not transfer to natural reading, for structural reasons: RSVP makes regressions impossible (the 10–15% of eye movements that are backward saccades, the brain's automatic repair mechanism when syntax or meaning fails to integrate), and it abolishes parafoveal preview (pre-processing of the upcoming word in peripheral vision, which natural reading depends on).
