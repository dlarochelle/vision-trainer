Vision Trainer

Two intervals, cued 1 and 2. The flankers appear in both. The faint central target appears in exactly one. Answer with F/← for interval 1, J/→ for interval 2. Stop if you get a headache, eye strain, or double vision.

Score: contrast sensitivity function

Threshold contrast per spatial frequency, plotted as sensitivity (1/threshold). This is a demonstration of the paradigm at a small fraction of any studied training dose: published protocols run roughly 900–1,000 trials per session over 30–40 sessions; a block here is a few dozen trials.

Deviation from the published protocols, stated plainly: they do not sweep fixed spatial frequencies (Polat starts low and advances adaptively; Zhou et al. train at the individual's cutoff frequency). This app sweeps 3 / 6 / 12 c/deg because it is measuring a contrast sensitivity function, not replicating a training protocol.

Degrees are derived from an assumed 96 CSS px/inch display and the viewing distance above. Display scale magnifies the drawn stimulus for visibility; the px/degree readout stays at the true, unscaled value, so magnification is never disguised as angular size. Thresholds are in 8-bit pixel contrast, not gamma-linearized luminance; comparable within one display, not across displays.

This mode measures comprehension under RSVP (rapid serial visual presentation). It does not measure, and is not known to improve, natural reading. Rayner et al. (2016, Psychological Science in the Public Interest) concluded that RSVP gains do not transfer to natural reading, for structural reasons: RSVP makes regressions impossible (the 10–15% of eye movements that are backward saccades, the brain's automatic repair mechanism when syntax or meaning fails to integrate), and it abolishes parafoveal preview (pre-processing of the upcoming word in peripheral vision, which natural reading depends on).
